Applied Engineering Physics Syllabus

This subject covers crystallography, quantum mechanics, quantum computing, magnetic and dielectric materials, lasers, and fibre optics.

Unit-Wise Syllabus

  1. Unit I — Crystallography & Materials Characterization
    Unit cell, space lattice, basis, lattice parameters, crystal structures, Bravais lattices, packing factor of SC, BCC and FCC, Miller indices, inter-planar distance, defects in crystals, nanomaterials, X-ray diffraction, Bragg's law, powder method, and Debye Scherrer’s formula.
  2. Unit II — Quantum Mechanics
    de-Broglie hypothesis, Heisenberg uncertainty principle, wave function, postulates of quantum mechanics, operators, eigenvalues, eigenfunctions, expectation value, Schrödinger’s time-independent wave equation, particle in a one-dimensional box, Bloch’s theorem, Kronig-Penney model, energy bands, bandgap, and quantum confinement in nanomaterials.
  3. Unit III — Quantum Computing
    Linear algebra for quantum computation, Dirac’s Bra and Ket notation, Hilbert space, Bloch sphere, quantum computer, classical bits, qubits, multiple-qubit systems, quantum measurements, entanglement, quantum gates, and Deutsch-Jozsa, Shor, and Grover algorithms.
  4. Unit IV — Magnetic and Dielectric Materials
    Magnetic moment, classification of magnetic materials, hysteresis, Weiss domain theory, soft and hard magnetic materials, ferrimagnetic materials, magnetic hyperthermia, magnets for EVs, and Giant Magneto Resistance devices. Dielectric materials, polarization, ferroelectric, piezoelectric, pyroelectric materials, Fe-RAM, load cells, and fire sensors.
  5. Unit V — Lasers and Fiber Optics
    Characteristics of lasers, Einstein coefficients, metastable state, population inversion, pumping, lasing action, Ruby laser, He-Ne laser, CO₂ laser, semiconductor diode laser, barcode scanners, and LIDAR. Fibre optics, total internal reflection, construction of optical fibre, acceptance angle, numerical aperture, fibre losses, and applications.

Quick Applied Engineering Physics Notes

Bragg’s Law

Bragg’s law explains X-ray diffraction in crystals: nλ = 2d sinθ.

Qubit

A qubit is the basic unit of quantum information. It can represent 0, 1, or a combination of both states.

Laser

LASER means Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation. It produces coherent and focused light.

Optical Fibre

Optical fibre carries light using total internal reflection and is used in communication systems.

After Completing AEP, You Will Be Able To

  • Explain crystal structures, X-ray diffraction, and nanomaterials.
  • Apply basic concepts of quantum mechanics and energy bands.
  • Understand qubits, quantum gates, and quantum algorithms.
  • Describe magnetic and dielectric materials used in engineering.
  • Explain the working of lasers and optical fibre communication.

Sample AEP Experiment

Numerical Aperture of an Optical Fibre

Aim: To determine the numerical aperture of an optical fibre.

  1. Connect the optical fibre to a light source.
  2. Place a screen at a known distance from the fibre end.
  3. Measure the diameter of the light spot on the screen.
  4. Calculate the acceptance angle using the measurements.
  5. Find numerical aperture using: NA = sin θ.
